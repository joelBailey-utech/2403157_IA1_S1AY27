a. My Top Three Strongest Traits & Hobbies

1. Discipline

Trait

Importance: Discipline keeps me consistent even when motivation fades. It's the bridge between goals and achievement.

Impact on academics: I wake up early, follow a study timetable, and submit assignments before deadlines. This trait has earned me steady grades and reduced last-minute stress during exams.

2. Curiosity

Trait

Importance: Curiosity pushes me beyond the syllabus. I ask "why" and "how" instead of just memorising facts.

Impact on academics: It led me to explore CSS, Python, and web development outside class. Curiosity turns assignments into learning opportunities rather than chores.

3. Coding & Problem-Solving

Hobby

Importance: Building small projects teaches patience, logic, and creativity — skills that go far beyond the screen.

Impact on academics: Debugging code trains me to troubleshoot calmly, which carries over into tackling difficult coursework. It also gives me a practical outlet for what I learn in CIT2011.

Bonus: Reading & Reflection

Hobby

Importance: Reading (like The Richest Man in Babylon) and journaling help me think deeply and process emotions.

Impact on academics: Improved vocabulary, focus, and the ability to write reflective essays with clarity.

Emotional Impact

Emotions like frustration when code won't run, and joy when it finally works, have taught me emotional resilience. I've learned that emotions are data — they signal what I care about and where I need to grow. Balancing them helps me stay productive in group work and exams.

b. My Hero: Jesus Christ

Background

Jesus of Nazareth is the central figure of Christianity, believed by Christians to be the Son of God and the Messiah. He lived roughly 2,000 years ago in the region of Judea and Galilee. His life, teachings, death, and resurrection are recorded in the Gospels of the New Testament. He was a teacher, healer, and servant-leader whose message of love, forgiveness, and humility has shaped billions of lives across two millennia.

Why He Is My Hero

Jesus is my hero because He modelled a life of selfless love and humility — even when He had every right to demand power. He washed His disciples' feet, dined with outcasts, forgave His enemies, and gave His life for people who rejected Him. In a world that often teaches "look out for number one," He taught the opposite: whoever wants to be great must first be a servant.

As a student, His example challenges me to be patient with difficult group members, honest even when cheating would be easier, and humble enough to ask for help when I don't understand something. He reminds me that character matters more than grades.

Favourite Quote

"For what shall it profit a man, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?"
— Jesus Christ, Mark 8:36 (KJV)

This verse is my personal motto. It reminds me that success without integrity is empty. No grade, job, or salary is worth compromising my values or my relationship with God.

Favourite Scene

My favourite scene is in the Garden of Gethsemane, where Jesus prayed in anguish before His arrest (Luke 22:42–44). He was fully human — sweating, afraid, and asking if there was another way — yet He still chose to obey God's will. That scene shows me that courage isn't the absence of fear, it's choosing to do what's right while afraid. I carry that lesson into every exam, presentation, and difficult decision.

c. Critical Essay: "LLMs Must Be Used as a Tool, Not a Crutch"

A student's reflection on AI in learning

Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot have transformed how students learn. They can explain hard concepts, generate examples, and help debug code in seconds. But the statement "LLMs must be used as a tool, not a crutch" draws an essential line. A tool amplifies your own ability; a crutch replaces it. As a student, I believe LLMs should strengthen my thinking, never substitute it.

What the Statement Means to Me

A tool is something a skilled person uses to do better work — like a calculator in a maths exam, or a spell-checker in an essay. A crutch is something you lean on because you cannot stand without it. If I use ChatGPT to write my essay, I've traded my learning for a shortcut. If I use it to brainstorm ideas and then write in my own voice, I've used a tool. The difference is whether I'm still doing the thinking.

When, Where, and How I Use LLMs

  • When: After I've attempted a problem myself and I'm stuck — not before I've tried.
  • Where: For studying concepts (e.g., "explain CSS Flexbox with an analogy"), planning outlines, checking grammar, and debugging code snippets.
  • How: I always verify facts against textbooks and cite sources. I rewrite AI explanations in my own words to confirm I actually understand.

Where I Draw the Line

I do not use LLMs to:

  • Submit AI-written assignments as my own work (this is academic dishonesty).
  • Skip the struggle of problem-solving — that struggle is where real learning happens.
  • Replace my own critical thinking or reflection.

Conclusion

LLMs are powerful learning partners, but only if I remain the pilot. Used as a tool, they deepen understanding, save time, and open doors to new knowledge. Used as a crutch, they weaken the very skills I'm in school to build. The statement is not a warning against AI — it's a call to maturity. I choose to use AI wisely, honestly, and always in service of my own growth.
