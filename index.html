Chapter Reflection: 7 Cures for a Lean Purse

In this chapter, George S. Clason outlines seven timeless principles for building wealth, delivered through the character Arkad:

  1. Start thy purse to fattening=> save at least 10% of what you earn.
  2. Control thy expenditures =>live below your means.
  3. Make thy gold multiply =>put savings to work through investment.
  4. Guard thy treasures from loss =>invest wisely, avoid risky schemes.
  5. Make of thy dwelling a profitable investment =>own your home.
  6. Insure a future income =>plan for retirement and family security.
  7. Increase thy ability to earn =>continuously improve your skills.

How I Align This With My Life

As a student, I apply the first cure by setting aside a small portion of any allowance or freelance income into a savings account. The seventh cure resonates most every lab, tutorial, and project I complete in CIT2011 is an investment in my earning ability. I also practice the second cure by budgeting for books and tools instead of impulsive spending.

Chapter Reflection: 5 Laws of Gold

The "Five Laws of Gold" expand on the earlier chapter with deeper principles:

  1. Gold comes gladly to those who save at least a tenth of their earnings.
  2. Gold works for the wise owner who finds profitable employment for it.
  3. Gold clings to the cautious owner who invests with wise counsel.
  4. Gold slips away from those who invest in unfamiliar ventures or schemes.
  5. Gold flees from those who seek impossible returns.

How I Align This With My Life

These laws have taught me patience. As a student, I am tempted to compare myself to peers who may seem ahead. Clason's wisdom reminds me that slow, disciplined progress is the foundation of lasting success. I apply the second law by investing my time in high-value learning (like mastering CSS) rather than distraction.

My Biggest Takeaway

My biggest takeaway from The Richest Man in Babylon is that wealth is a habit, not an event. Whether managing money or managing study time, the same discipline applies: save first, invest wisely, and keep improving. I now treat my education as my first "gold" something to protect, cultivate, and multiply.

I encourage every student to read this book; its principles outlive generations and apply far beyond finance.
